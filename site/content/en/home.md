## Why

- **Anyone, anywhere.** Cheap parts, found all over the world, new or
  salvaged from old telephones.
- **More people.** Each build suits different people; the phone exchange
  could serve those who cannot see a screen.
- **Nobody in between.** A commercial signer can be tampered with on its way
  to you. One you build yourself gives an attacker no shipment to target.

## How it started

Blame a pop song. In ["Catch Catch"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NOiyDlWl534),
K-pop singer Yena uses the beeps of a telephone keypad as part of the music.
Somewhere between the second chorus and the third replay, a very unmusical
question came up: if a song can carry DTMF, why not a Bitcoin transaction?
Add the freshly finished [NDS-Signer](https://github.com/ndssigner/nds-signer),
which had just taught a Nintendo DSi to sign, and DTMF-Signer was the obvious
next step. So yes: this signer exists because someone could not stop
replaying a song. No regrets.
